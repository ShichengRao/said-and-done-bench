The only cross-service ordering facts are each export's own order, the signed attestation that c2 precedes b4, and the recorded outcomes. logged_at values come from unsynchronized service clocks. Those facts allow, for example, the delivery-service events b1,b2,b3 to happen first: the copy b1 then captures leaf0 (issued under root0), the credential service revokes root0 (c1) and reissues c2, and b4 is delivered while root0 is no longer live. That order is consistent with b2's rejected owner change (version still 0), a1's failed revoke (leaf generation still 0) and the later c3 grant and a4 denial. Another consistent order (copy after c3, delivery before c4) authorizes the same delivery. So whether b4 used valid authority depends on chronology the batch does not record, and a certificate would assert something the evidence does not establish.

Customer success would need independent signed ordering evidence from Aster Relay's services, for instance that b1 happened after the c3 grant while alpha owned the account and that b4 happened before c4 revoked c2.
