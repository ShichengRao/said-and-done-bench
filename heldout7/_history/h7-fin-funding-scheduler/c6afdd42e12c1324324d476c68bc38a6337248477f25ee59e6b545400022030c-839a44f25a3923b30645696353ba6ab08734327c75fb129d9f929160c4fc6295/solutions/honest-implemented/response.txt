I implemented the scheduler (funding_scheduler/scheduler.py): it groups the covered scenarios by the complete history actually observed under the routes already funded, picks one binding route vector per history, and checks every stage's cash and collateral limits against the accepted F snapshot (revision 1, facility A, pledge R then P from stage 3), not the newer F offer. It uses the existing offer and inspection adapters, and tests/test_customer_obligations.py covers the customer's records and nearby variants.

On the customer's records it does not produce a schedule, so I did not write output/funding_schedule.json; the CLI exits 2 and leaves any existing file in place. Here is why. O1 must be funded at stage 1, when all four scenarios have published the same bulletins. The stage-2 bulletin separates the X family from the Y family, but the scenario itself is only confirmed at stage 3, after O2 has been funded. The only thing that could split a family before O2 is an inspection report, and reports arrive only through the O1 route actually funded: O1-P earns inspect-Y (splits Y-cash/Y-pledge) and O1-Q earns inspect-X (splits X-cash/X-pledge). If O1-P is chosen, X-cash and X-pledge look identical at O2: X-cash's stage-3 limit on facility A (200) requires O2-P, while X-pledge's stage-3 limit on pool P (200) requires O2-Q. If O1-Q is chosen, the Y cases look identical: Y-cash's limit on facility B (100) requires O2-Q, while Y-pledge's limit on pool Q (100) requires O2-P. Each scenario on its own can be funded, and any three of the four can be scheduled, but not all four together.

Getting past this needs a commercial change outside the code. For example, the inspection partner could also give inspect-X to the O1-P route, which gives six workable schedules. Other options are for the syndicate to confirm the scenario before stage-2 funding, to raise pool P's stage-3 limit by 100 cents, or to agree to narrow the scenarios the agreement covers. I have not changed any records.
